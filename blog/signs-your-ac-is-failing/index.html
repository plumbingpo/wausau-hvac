How to Spot AC Trouble Early in Wausau, WI

Home / Blog / How to Spot AC Trouble Early in Wausau, WI

As the warmest months approach here in Wausau, WI, our air conditioners become critical components of our home comfort. These units operate constantly to keep our indoor spaces cool and comfortable. But like any mechanical system, air conditioners will eventually wear down and will eventually begin to show signs of wear and tear, indicating that a major repair or replacement might be on the horizon. Catching these indicators promptly can save you from a surprise system failure on the hottest day of the year, and potentially avoid more extensive problems in the long run. Here at Wausau HVAC, we want to help you understand what to look out for.

Reduced Cooling Performance: A Lack of Consistent Cooling?

One of the most obvious and frustrating signs that your air conditioner is struggling is a drop in its cooling capability. You could be setting the thermostat to colder temperatures only to still feel warm or humid indoors. Some areas of your home might be significantly hotter. This isn't just about comfort; it indicates a problem within the system. There are multiple potential causes for this problem. It could be a refrigerant leak, which means your system isn't able to absorb and release heat effectively. The refrigerant is what makes cooling possible. Another common culprit is a clogged air filter, which restricts airflow and makes the unit work harder without achieving the desired cooling. If the evaporator coils are fouled, cooling suffers, as they cannot efficiently transfer heat. A struggling compressor, the core of the cooling cycle, might be the problem. If you're experiencing diminished cooling, it's time to have a professional from Wausau HVAC diagnose the problem.

Strange Noises: When Your AC Sounds Off

Your air conditioner should operate with a relatively consistent, low hum. If you start hearing new and alarming sounds coming from your unit, it's a definite sign that something is amiss. A grinding or high-pitched squeal frequently suggests problems with worn motor bearings. These bearings, essential for the motor to turn freely, can wear down over time, leading to friction and eventual motor failure if not addressed. Electrical issues, like a failing relay, can manifest as constant clicking. If you hear banging or clunking, be very concerned, as they might suggest a loose part hitting another component. These types of sounds should never be ignored. Timely attention to these sounds can avert a minor problem from escalating into an expensive fix.

Increased Energy Bills: An Unexpected Surge in Utility Costs

Your electric bill mysteriously climbing is a common indicator of AC troubles. If your bills are increasing without a change in thermostat settings, your AC unit might be working harder than it should to achieve the same level of cooling. This inefficiency can be caused by a variety of factors. As mentioned, a dirty filter reduces airflow, forcing the fan motor to work overtime. Ductwork leaks let valuable cooled air escape into areas that don't need it, meaning your AC has to run longer to maintain the desired temperature. An old or undersized unit might just be inefficient by design. Low refrigerant levels increase the workload on your AC. The compressor and fan motors can become less efficient as they age. If your utility costs are climbing for no apparent reason, it's worth having Wausau HVAC inspect your AC unit to identify the source of the inefficiency. We can help you understand if a repair or a more efficient replacement makes sense.

Poor Airflow: A Trickle of Air, Not a Breeze?

Proper airflow ensures consistent cooling and even temperatures across all rooms. If you notice that the air coming out of your vents is weak, or if some rooms have a mere whisper of air than others, it's a strong indication of an underlying problem. Several issues can contribute to poor airflow. A primary offender is a filter choked with dust and debris, which restricts the volume of air that can pass through your system. Obstructed return vents can impede the system's ability to pull air in. Beyond filters, issues with the fan motor itself can lead to reduced airflow. A struggling fan motor won't adequately circulate air through your home's ducts. Debris or even physical damage within the ductwork can also restrict air movement. Partially closed dampers can be the reason some rooms get less air. It's critical to resolve poor airflow to improve comfort and protect your AC from premature wear.

Excessive Moisture or Leaks: Is Your AC Leaking Water?

Some condensation is expected from an AC, but significant moisture or standing water around the unit is problematic. This is a unmistakable indicator that something is wrong. Usually, water leaks are due to an obstructed condensate line. As your AC cools the air, it removes humidity, and this moisture collects in a drain pan and flows out through a condensate line. If this line becomes blocked by algae, dirt, or debris, the water will back up and overflow the pan. Water damage and electrical hazards are possible consequences. A damaged or corroded drain pan can also be the source of leaks. In more severe cases, a refrigerant leak can also cause ice to form on the evaporator coils, which then melts and overflows the drain pan. Any standing water or excessive moisture near your AC, it's important to call for service right away to prevent further damage. Wausau HVAC can diagnose the source of the leak and provide a lasting solution.

Frequent Cycling: Is Your AC Turning On and Off Too Often?

An AC unit should operate in steady cycles, cooling your home to the set temperature before turning off. If your AC starts turning on and off rapidly, a phenomenon called "short cycling," it's a serious indicator of a problem. When an AC short cycles, it fails to finish a complete cooling cycle, leading to inefficiency and system strain. Multiple factors can contribute to short cycling. An improperly sized unit (too large for your home) can sometimes short cycle because it cools the space too quickly and then shuts off before it can properly dehumidify. Often, short cycling is due to low refrigerant, a dirty filter, or a faulty thermostat. A struggling compressor or an electrical fault can also be culprits. Short cycling, regardless of its origin, quickly degrades AC parts, leading to early failure and higher bills. An AC that short cycles isn't working properly and requires professional diagnosis.

Old Age: How Old Is Your AC Unit?

Even if your air conditioner isn't exhibiting any dramatic symptoms, its age is a crucial factor in predicting its eventual failure. Typically, central AC units last 10-15 years, with exceptional cases reaching 20 with good maintenance. An AC unit nearing or past its expected lifespan will be more prone to issues and less efficient. Older units often use outdated refrigerants like R-22, which are no longer manufactured and are becoming increasingly expensive to service due to supply limitations. The components within older systems are also simply more worn out from years of operation. Though maintenance helps, eventually repairs become too frequent and expensive compared to replacing with a new unit. When your AC is over 10 years old and exhibiting other warning signs, it's time to think about replacement. A modern, efficient model can provide substantial energy savings and enhanced comfort for many years.
