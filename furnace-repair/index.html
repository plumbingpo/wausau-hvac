A modern furnace runs a strict startup sequence, and knowing where the sequence breaks is most of the diagnosis. The thermostat calls, the inducer motor spins up to prove draft through a pressure switch, the hot surface igniter glows, the gas valve opens, the burners light, the flame sensor confirms fire, and only then does the blower deliver heat to the house. In Wausau, WI, the failures cluster at predictable points: a flame sensor coated in oxide that can no longer prove the flame, a hot surface igniter cracked from years of thermal cycling, a pressure switch that will not close because the inducer is weak or the condensate trap on a high-efficiency model has clogged, or a control board with a burned relay. Each one stops the sequence cold, and each one mimics the others from the hallway.

Rather than guess, Wausau HVAC tests the sequence stage by stage. We read igniter resistance, measure flame signal in microamps, check that the pressure switch responds to real inducer draft, confirm voltage at the gas valve, and test the blower and its capacitor. Because this is a fuel-burning appliance, the safety basics come with every visit: the heat exchanger gets inspected, gas connections get leak-tested, and draft gets verified. You cannot tell a furnace problem from a furnace hazard by looking at the thermostat. Before we leave, temperature rise is measured against the data plate so airflow and flame are matched as the manufacturer intended.

Conventional or condensing, gas, propane, or electric, we repair them all, across every major brand, including the traps, drains, and dual pressure switches that come with high-efficiency models. The parts most likely to fail are stocked on every truck: igniters, flame sensors, pressure switches, capacitors, inducer assemblies, and blower motors. When to Call for Furnace Repair in Wausau

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. These are the signals our technicians see most often when a problem is underway:

  • Clicking and a glowing igniter with no burner flame
  • Burners light, run a few seconds, then shut down repeatedly
  • A bang or boom when the burners ignite late
  • Frequent on-off cycling that never reaches the thermostat setting
  • Soot, rust, or moisture near the furnace cabinet or vent pipe
  • A fan that keeps running while the registers blow cool air
  • Squealing or grinding from the blower compartment
  • A flame that burns soft and yellow rather than steady blue

Why Professional Furnace Repair Matters

Gas, open flame, high voltage, and a flue all share one cabinet in a furnace, and the most dangerous failure is the one you cannot see: a damaged heat exchanger that lets combustion gases into the air the blower sends through your home. Every morning that delayed ignition keeps booming at startup, that exchanger takes more stress. The diagnostic side has its own pitfalls. When a furnace trips its high limit, the switch itself may be bad, or the furnace may be protecting itself from a clogged filter or a failing blower. Replacing the switch when the real issue is airflow removes a safety device and leaves the actual problem in place.

Our experienced technicians treat every symptom as a question about the whole system. Flame signal gets measured rather than assumed, so a healthy sensor is never replaced while a cracked igniter or weak gas valve skates by. Draft is verified at the pressure switch and the flue. Temperature rise is checked against the data plate, which is how airflow problems get caught before they shorten the exchanger's life. And the gas side is leak-tested with instruments, not a sniff and a shrug. The result is a furnace that has been proven safe and correct under a full cycle, and an honest report if the unit is nearing the point where replacement beats another repair.

Maintenance Tips for Furnace Repair

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly heating and cooling problems down the road. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Put in a fresh filter monthly through the heating season, the cheapest protection your furnace can get
  • Give the furnace room to breathe, since a crowded utility space starves the burners of air
  • Pay attention when the burners light, since a boom instead of a soft whoosh calls for service
  • Clear boxes, lint, and above all chlorine-based cleaners out of the furnace area
  • Keep a working carbon monoxide alarm on every level of the home and check it each month
  • If you have a high-efficiency furnace, check that the condensate drain is flowing freely
  • Have the furnace checked every autumn, while a weak igniter or fouled sensor is still an easy fix
