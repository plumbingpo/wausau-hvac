Heating and Air Conditioning Service Across Madison

Heating and cooling around here covers a lot of ground: aging furnaces in established neighborhoods, high-efficiency heat pumps in recent construction, ductless mini-splits in additions and converted garages, and packaged units on commercial buildings. Our team handles all of it. We have years of field experience, and we've learned how the weather here, the housing stock, and code requirements affect the way equipment performs over time.

Most homeowners we meet need one of three things: a repair, a replacement, or regular maintenance. Repairs tend to be straightforward: a burned contactor, a corroded flame sensor, a refrigerant leak, or a seized blower motor. Replacements get a proper load calculation, not a guess based on the old unit's tonnage, which keeps temperatures even and stops the new equipment from short-cycling its way to an early failure. Tune-ups include coil cleaning, a refrigerant charge check, safety control testing, and a static pressure reading, and nothing does more to extend the life of your system.

A furnace that keeps short-cycling, or any burning smell, means it's time to kill power at the furnace switch and call us right away.

Getting More Years From Your HVAC System in Madison

Routine maintenance is the difference between a system that runs for years and one that breaks down in the middle of a heat wave. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a new filter every one to three months, two feet of clearance around the outdoor condenser, a gentle rinse each spring for the condenser coil, and two professional tune-ups a year, so small issues get caught early.

Age matters too. Most central air conditioners last 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15 with regular service. If your system is past that window and starting to struggle, such as climbing utility bills, rooms that never feel comfortable, or a service call every season, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and far less stressful than an emergency one in the middle of a cold snap.

The earlier you report a comfort problem, the cheaper it usually is. A capacitor that's starting to fail is an easy, low-cost part to replace, yet the same part, neglected through a full season, can ruin the compressor and turn a simple fix into a full system replacement. Save our number on your phone now so help is one call away the moment something seems off in your home. A quick call saves time, money, and sleepless nights.
