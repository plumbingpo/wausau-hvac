Why Hire a Professional for AC Installation?

The worst installation mistakes are the ones you cannot see at startup. Skip a proper vacuum, and moisture left in the line set turns to acid that attacks the compressor over the next few summers. Guess at the refrigerant charge instead of weighing it in, and the system wastes efficiency every hour it runs. Pick the size by rule of thumb, and an oversized unit short-cycles toward an early failure while the house stays humid. Each of these problems shows up later as the very repair bills and high energy costs a new system was supposed to end.

The steps that make equipment last are not flashy, and our technicians do every one of them. The line set is pressure-tested with nitrogen before evacuation. The vacuum is verified on a micron gauge. The charge is weighed in, then confirmed against superheat and subcooling. The condensate drain gets a proper trap and overflow protection, and airflow is measured with static pressure instead of a hand over a vent. Refrigerant work is also federally regulated and requires recovery and evacuation equipment you will not find at a hardware store. How to Prevent AC Installation Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly heating and cooling problems down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Change the filter on schedule from day one so the new coil never sees starved airflow
  • Keep setpoints steady and moderate rather than making the system recover from big swings in peak heat
  • Have a technician check the system every spring so charge and amp draws stay where they were set
  • Leave all supply and return registers open so airflow stays balanced the way it was set up
  • Fix minor problems early, since a weak capacitor found in spring will not strand you in summer
  • Flush the condensate line each spring so the float switch never has to save you
  • Keep grass, shrubs, and debris clear of the condenser, and rinse its coil when it gets dirty
